# Design prompt: redesign of www.jackzhaojin.com

Paste this into a design tool or send it to a designer as is. It is self-contained. Companion documents in this repo hold the full reasoning ([seo-site-plan.md](seo-site-plan.md), [seo-page-blueprint.md](seo-page-blueprint.md)); nothing below depends on them.

## Prompt

You are designing the personal website of Jack Jin: Associate Director at Accenture, cloud architect, Adobe Certified Master, AEM Champion 2026, conference speaker (adaptTo 2026 Berlin, Columbus AI Week). He builds AI agent systems and works at the intersection of Adobe Experience Manager (AEM), Edge Delivery Services (EDS) and AI. Look at the current site, especially https://www.jackzhaojin.com/portfolio/, for the voice and the diagram style already in use.

**Purpose of the site.** Be the place Google, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude cite for "AEM + AI" and agentic engineering, so conference organizers, clients and peers find him. Three audiences at once: humans reading on a phone, search engines, and AI answer engines that read raw HTML.

**Voice.** An engineer's plain voice. The work carries the page; copy points at it. No hype, no hero slogans, no filler adjectives, no em dashes. Numbers come from git history and are shown as facts. Honest limitations stay in.

**Content model.** Posts (articles, short posts, videos, 39 today and growing), projects (12), talks (5), certifications (25), four topic hubs (AEM + AI flagship, Adobe AEM and EDS, AI Agents, Working with AI), an About page with a speaker kit.

### Pages to design, in priority order

1. Home `/`
2. Topic hub `/aem-ai/`
3. Post detail `/blog/{slug}/`, in two states: article and video (embed, chapters, transcript)
4. Project detail `/portfolio/{slug}/`
5. Blog collection `/blog/` with filters and pagination
6. About `/about/` with speaker kit and contact

Desktop and mobile for each. Include header, footer and breadcrumb.

### Ordered content per page (do not reorder, may restyle)

- **Home:** H1 "Jack Jin" with one-line positioning; 40 to 60 word summary; four hub cards; latest three posts; one featured project; credentials strip; recent and upcoming talks; speaking and contact call to action.
- **Hub:** breadcrumb; H1 with the topic name; 40 to 60 word definition; "Start here" list of 3 to 5 pieces with a reason each; counts (posts, videos, projects, talks); sections for Projects, Videos, Articles and posts, Talks; related hubs; FAQ of 3 to 5 questions.
- **Post:** breadcrumb; H1; byline (author, published, updated, read or watch time, format); 40 to 60 word summary as the first paragraph; key facts table; body with question-shaped H2s, numbered steps, comparison tables; for video, the embed with a plain watch link, chapter list with timestamps, full transcript; FAQ; related links and "Discuss on LinkedIn"; author box.
- **Project:** breadcrumb; H1; summary; facts table (repo, status, first and last commit, commits, tags, stack, build method); architecture diagram; sections for what it does, how it is built, what it proved, limitations; related posts and talks.
- **Blog collection:** breadcrumb; H1; summary; featured intro; counts by facet; filters (topic buttons are links to hubs; format and type toggles; sort); card grid newest first; numbered pagination; FAQ.
- **About:** breadcrumb; H1; bio summary; headshot; facts table; speaker bios at 50, 100 and 200 words; speaking topics; past talks; credentials; contact (email and LinkedIn links); profile links.

### Hard constraints, with the reason

- All content above exists in the initial HTML; scripts may animate or filter but never supply content. Reason: AI engines and most crawlers do not run JavaScript.
- One H1 per page; H2 and H3 in order; headings read like questions people ask. Reason: headings are how engines map a page.
- Facts are real HTML tables, steps are ordered lists. Reason: parsers lift tables and lists verbatim.
- Tabs, accordions and carousels are allowed only if the content stays in the DOM and the default view shows the summary, the facts table and the first section. Reason: hidden content is discounted.
- The summary paragraph sits directly under the H1 and byline with no hero image between. Reason: the first passage is what gets quoted.
- Breadcrumb directly above the H1, mirroring the URL. Reason: orientation for people, structure for engines.
- Every diagram has one-sentence alt text naming what it shows; no fact lives only inside an image. Reason: accessibility and machine readability.
- Dates (published, updated) visible near the H1. Reason: freshness is weighted heavily by AI engines.
- Use "Adobe Experience Manager (AEM)", "Edge Delivery Services (EDS)", "DA.live" in headings, never "Adobe Experience Cloud". Reason: those are the searched terms.
- Header nav: AEM + AI, Blog, Portfolio, Talks, Certifications, About, plus a "Book a talk" button. Footer: Topics, Sections, Elsewhere (LinkedIn, GitHub, YouTube, Credly), Machine files (llms.txt, sitemap, privacy).
- Contact is an email link and a LinkedIn link, not a JavaScript form. Reason: agents act through links.
- Mobile first; no horizontal scroll; tables scroll inside their own container.

### Sample content to design with

Post (video): "Adobe Stardust Explained: Design Agent Skills on Edge Delivery Services". Published 2026-08-09. Watch time 12 minutes. Summary: what Stardust is, how design agent skills generate and theme EDS sites, what worked and what did not in a hands-on run. Facts: topic AEM + AI; format video; tools Adobe EDS, Stardust, Claude Code; related project Content Factory; also on LinkedIn and YouTube. Chapters: What Stardust is (0:00), Setting up the skill (2:10), Theming a new site (5:30), Where it broke (9:40), Takeaways (11:15).

Project: "Anima Mesh". Cloud-first, one private brain per instance; nine agents, four coordination approaches; safety lives in code, not prompts. Facts: repo on GitHub, status active, 2026, build method spec-first.

### Deliverables

Mockups for the six pages at desktop and mobile; a design token sheet (type scale, spacing, color with light and dark); a component inventory (card, facts table, byline, breadcrumb, FAQ, embed with fallback link, pagination, filter bar); and, if the tool supports it, HTML and CSS for the post detail page. Explain any place where you deliberately deviate from a constraint and which audience pays for it.
